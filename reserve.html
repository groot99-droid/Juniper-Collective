Our work

The Reserve at Hot Springs

A Gilded Age mansion in Hot Springs, Arkansas, restored and opened as a twelve-room inn, wedding venue and place to hold a retreat. Two of our founders were part of the family team that brought it back.

Visit the Reserve Book a diagnostic

The place

A landmark that had to earn its keep

The W.C. Brown House has stood in the middle of Hot Springs for more than a hundred and thirty years. It began as a Queen Anne house, was remodeled in 1919 into a Neoclassical residence, and was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1986.

Today it is the centerpiece of an inn on four acres, with guest rooms in the main house, the Legacy House beside it and a private cottage suite. Breakfast draws on the property's own greenhouse and gardens. The grounds hold weddings, meetings and retreats.

A house like this can be loved and still lose money. The work was to turn a landmark into a business guests return to, without losing what made the house worth visiting.

What a place like this has to get right

Three things, learned the hard way

The house

Restoring a landmark is a promise to the building. The plan has to protect it while making room for the guests who will pay for it.

The guest

Breakfast, linens, a porch at the right hour. Guests remember how a place treated them, and the kitchen is where that is kept or lost.

The numbers

Rooms, weddings, meetings and retreats each earn differently. A good plan decides which to lead with and prices them to match.

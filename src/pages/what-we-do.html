What we do

The whole arc of a place

Most firms hold one piece. We hold all of it: what the place should become, who it is for, how it reaches the world, and how it stays true once the doors open.

The arc

Most firms handle one piece

One firm designs the brand. Another builds. A third runs the launch. Each piece can be sound, and the whole can still cost you twice what it should.

Here is how it goes wrong. The price you set decides who arrives. Who arrives decides how the place has to feel. How it feels decides whether they come back. Change one without the others and the money leaks out the gaps.

So we start with one question, before any deliverable: what should this become, and for whom? Identity, pricing, audience, pre-launch marketing and the opening are that answer, worked out in order.

A place is not made on opening day. It is kept.

Four phases

From the first idea to the first busy season

I
Phase I

Conception & positioning

Before ground is broken, you decide what the place is. We set the core commercial ethos, define how each property differs from its neighbors and from the other properties in your portfolio, and draft the initial architectural thesis. This is the moment when being certain costs the least.

Likely deliverables

  • {{icon:check}}Commercial ethos statement
  • {{icon:check}}Property differentiation map
  • {{icon:check}}Initial architectural thesis
II
Phase II

Identity & pricing architecture

Identity and price are one decision seen from two sides. We model yield, build the visual system, write the spatial vernacular and segment guests into tiers, so that what a place looks like, what it costs and whom it welcomes all agree. Where they disagree, guests feel it before they can name it.

Likely deliverables

  • {{icon:check}}Yield model
  • {{icon:check}}Visual identity system
  • {{icon:check}}Tiered guest segmentation
III
Phase III

Pre-launch demand

A place that opens to silence has opened late. We build private interest lists, write the editorial narrative, host founder salons and gather pre-booking momentum, so the first guests are expected rather than discovered. Demand is not announced at the door; it is cultivated before the door exists.

Likely deliverables

  • {{icon:check}}Private interest list
  • {{icon:check}}Editorial narrative
  • {{icon:check}}Founder salon program
IV
Phase IV

Opening & stewardship

The opening is a threshold, not a finish line. We stage the on-site experience, set the operational rituals that keep it consistent, and remain on retainer to govern design as the place settles into daily operation. Intent is easy to state on day one and easy to lose by the first busy season.

Likely deliverables

  • {{icon:check}}Opening experience staging plan
  • {{icon:check}}Operational ritual set
  • {{icon:check}}Design governance retainer

The doctrine

The Juniper Method

A portfolio is not a list of properties; it is a single argument made in many places.

  • One thesis that holds the space, the guest and the commerce together.
  • One portfolio, so every property reads as a node of the same world.
  • One steward after opening, so the intent survives the operation.
